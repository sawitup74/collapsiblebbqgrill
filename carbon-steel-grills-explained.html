Carbon Steel Grills Explained

What carbon steel is, why it is used for collapsible grills, how it differs from stainless, and why it needs seasoning.

Carbon steel is iron with a small amount of carbon and very little else. It is the same family of metal as a carbon steel wok or a blacksmith's skillet: strong, easy to cut precisely, tolerant of high heat, and able to take on seasoning. Both Slot Grill models are carbon steel; the makers state plainly there is no stainless steel version.

Why use it for a grill

  • Strength at thickness. Thick carbon steel stays rigid when it is hot and loaded with coals and cast iron.
  • Heat. It shrugs off the heat of a charcoal or wood fire.
  • Seasoning. Oil heated onto carbon steel polymerizes into a dark, slick coating that protects it and helps food release.
  • Repairable finish. If it rusts on the surface, you scrub and re-season. There is no coating to chip off for good.

Carbon steel vs stainless

Carbon steelStainless steel
RustRusts if left wet and bare; seasoning protects itResists rust
CareKeep oiled and dryWipe and go
SeasoningTakes seasoning wellDoesn't hold seasoning the same way
Look over timeDarkens to a patinaDiscolors with heat

The trade is simple: carbon steel asks for a minute of oil after a cook, and in return you get thick, strong steel that you can restore with a scrub pad. See seasoning a new grill and dealing with surface rust.

Body vs cooking surfaces

Slot Grill's own FAQ says the makers like high-temperature BBQ paint on the grill body and keep the grilling surfaces lightly oiled. Their Seasoning Balm page also describes the balm as a food-safe alternative to paint for the body. Never put paint on grates or griddle plates.

Fire safety on any collapsible grill

  • Outdoors only. Burning charcoal makes carbon monoxide, which you can't see or smell. Never use it in a garage, tent, camper, vehicle, boat cabin or under a roof or awning.
  • Non-combustible, level ground. Soil, gravel, sand, concrete, pavers or a fire-safe pad.
  • Clearance. Away from walls, vehicles, tents, overhanging branches and dry grass. Kids and pets kept back.
  • Seated tabs. Check every tab is fully in its slot before you add fuel.
  • Water and gloves at hand. Hot steel looks exactly like cold steel.
  • Leave it alone until cold. Don't move or take apart a lit or warm grill.

Mistakes new flat pack owners make

  1. Too much charcoal. A small firebox gets very hot. Start with half a chimney and add.
  2. No cool zone. Without an empty end, a flare-up has nowhere to go.
  3. Cooking on black coals. Wait for gray ash.
  4. Old ash left in. It blocks airflow; empty it before every cook.
  5. Heavy oil when seasoning. Thin coats cure; thick coats turn gummy.
  6. Packing it warm. Wait until steel and ash are cold, every time.

Questions people ask

Is the Slot Grill stainless steel?

No. Both models are carbon steel, and there is no stainless version.

Will a carbon steel grill rust?

Bare, wet carbon steel will rust. Light surface rust is cosmetic: clean it off and re-season.
