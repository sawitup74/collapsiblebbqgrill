Steel Gauge and Why It Matters on a Portable Grill

What 10-gauge and 12-gauge mean, why thicker steel holds up better in a collapsible grill, and how gauge affects weight.

Sheet steel thickness is often given as a gauge number. The counterintuitive part: a lower gauge number means thicker steel. 10-gauge is thicker than 12-gauge, which is thicker than 16-gauge, and so on. Slot Grill's Full Size page puts 10-gauge in everyday terms: about the thickness of two stacked quarters.

Why thickness matters in a collapsible grill

  • Rigidity. A flat-pack grill's strength comes from the plates themselves. Thick plates don't flex when you load coals and a heavy pan.
  • Warping. Thin steel heated unevenly tends to warp. Thick steel is far more stable.
  • Heat holding. Thick steel stores heat, which helps a grate stay hot when cold food hits it.
  • Life span. Thin steel can burn through in a hot firebox over years of use; thick steel has more to give.

Fire safety on any collapsible grill

  • Outdoors only. Burning charcoal makes carbon monoxide, which you can't see or smell. Never use it in a garage, tent, camper, vehicle, boat cabin or under a roof or awning.
  • Non-combustible, level ground. Soil, gravel, sand, concrete, pavers or a fire-safe pad.
  • Clearance. Away from walls, vehicles, tents, overhanging branches and dry grass. Kids and pets kept back.
  • Seated tabs. Check every tab is fully in its slot before you add fuel.
  • Water and gloves at hand. Hot steel looks exactly like cold steel.
  • Leave it alone until cold. Don't move or take apart a lit or warm grill.

Mistakes new flat-pack owners make

  1. Too much charcoal. A small firebox gets very hot. Start with half a chimney and add.
  2. No cool zone. Without an empty end, a flare-up has nowhere to go.
  3. Cooking on black coals. Wait for gray ash.
  4. Old ash left in. It blocks airflow; empty it before every cook.
  5. Heavy oil when seasoning. Thin coats cure; thick coats turn gummy.
  6. Packing it warm. Wait until steel and ash are cold, every time.

Questions people ask

Is 10-gauge thicker than 12-gauge?

Yes. Lower gauge numbers mean thicker steel.
